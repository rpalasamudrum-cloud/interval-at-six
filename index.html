The day in Hindi cinema, drawn in panels

One edition a day. Six panels. The day’s Hindi film news — releases, box office, castings, the things that actually moved — drawn as a comic strip instead of written as a list.

The drawings are the point, but the sourcing is the promise: no panel goes out without a link to the outlet that reported it, and anything that is a forecast rather than a fact is labelled a projection on the panel itself.

How an edition is made

  • Six panels, chosen the same way every day. One lead, one on money, one on what releases next, one on the industry beyond the stars, one human story, one on streaming or television.
  • Sourced first, drawn second. The stories are pulled from the trade press on the morning of the edition. If a story cannot be sourced by the time the strip is drawn, it waits or it is dropped.
  • Projections are marked as projections. An opening-weekend estimate is not a box-office result and never gets written as one.
  • No photographs. Every panel is drawn — flat ink, halftone, one spot colour. Nobody’s likeness and nobody’s copyrighted still.
  • Some panels get no joke. If a story touches something that happened to real people, it runs quiet: no sound effect, no punchline.
