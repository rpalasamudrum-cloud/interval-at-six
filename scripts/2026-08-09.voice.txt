Nameet Malhotra has confirmed how Raamaayana goes out. International territories get it on the sixth of November. India gets it two days later, on the eighth — which is Diwaali.

Read that order again, because it is backwards. Almost every big Indian release opens at home first and lets the overseas run follow. This one opens abroad and holds India back.

There is a reason worth the two days. If you are releasing an adaptation of the Raamaayana, opening it in India on any date other than the festival of lights is leaving something on the table that money cannot buy. So the diaspora gets the film first, and India gets the occasion.

Whether the two-day gap survives contact with piracy is the interesting question, and nobody has answered it yet.
